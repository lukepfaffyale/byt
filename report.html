2Motivation

Who has the problem. Two groups, on two sides of the same trade.

  • People. Adults spend hours a day tapping and swiping on a phone. Published estimates for 2026 put the average at roughly four to five hours a day. Every tap tells someone what that person likes. That signal is sold, and the person is paid nothing and owns nothing at the end.
  • Organizations that need to know what people think. A brand testing a product, a recruiter looking for a kind of thinker, a school choosing a course. Today they buy a survey panel. On one large research panel the platform asks researchers to pay people at least 8 US dollars an hour and recommends 12, then adds a platform fee of about 43 percent for companies (33 percent for universities). It takes days, it reaches the people who like doing surveys, and each new question starts from zero.

What it costs them. The person gives away the one thing that will still be scarce when AI does most work: taste, judgment and feeling. The organization pays again for every question and still cannot tell whether an answer is how a person would truly act.

The idea. Let the person keep that signal in something they own: a Sym. The game builds it from taps, tests it against the person, and only then lets it answer for them. The Sym can be asked a thousand times while its person sleeps, and its person is paid each time. An AI system needs what is new and particular about people. BYT is a way for people to give it by choice and share in what it earns.

Eight things BYT does that the alternatives do not

  1. Proved, not claimed. A Sym commits its guesses before its person sees the cards. Plain code compares them and gives the certificate. No model can award one.
  2. A person checks before pay. When a Sym does a job, its person approves or corrects the answer first. A correction teaches the Sym.
  3. Groups, never one person. An agency sees totals over at least 5 Syms. It cannot type a question: it taps one from a fixed list.
  4. Three seats, one login. Anyone can be a player, run an agency and run a creator studio, and see each seat's own books.
  5. An economy where each seat wins when the others do. Agencies pay Syms for answers. Creators build the skill steps Syms grow on and earn a royalty on each pass. The game keeps 10 percent on top of what a seat spends.
  6. The Sym's code. Every input a person gives writes one block, in four letters like the bases of DNA: T a tap, G a proof, A an agency act, C a creator act. The game shows how each code differs from every other Sym's, as counts only.
  7. Worth is not weighed by money. Everyone in a group is paid the same. Asking the same group again costs more, and the extra goes to a growth fund that pays players for finishing skill steps.
  8. One map, kept true by tests. One file ties a tap to a letter, a style, an area of the mind, a skill and a certificate. Tests fail when the records and the code disagree.

4The agents

BYT has four agents. Each has one job, one read-only tool, and a check that is not a model. They never call each other. The database carries the hand-off, so the design has no loops between agents.

Model, and why

All four agents use gpt-6-luna through Portkey, on the Responses API. It is the model the course key allows, and at 0.10 US dollars per million input tokens it is cheap enough to run an agent every time a player finishes a gate. Each run is capped at 4 model requests and 3 tool calls. A daily token budget stops spending, and a practice Sym that uses no AI takes over when there is no key or no budget, so the game always works.

What a model never decides

Certificates, pay, prices, what an agency sees, the Sym's letters, its code and every line the Sym or the crew says on screen are plain code. When an agency asks 194 Syms a question, no model runs at all: each Sym answers from its person's counted picks. That keeps the answers free, repeatable and impossible to talk into something.

What was done to cut cost, and whether it hurt

  • The Twin reads beliefs, not raw picks. Eight short beliefs stand in for every pick the player made, so the Twin's input stays near 2,500 tokens however long the player has played.
  • The Profiler runs once per finished gate, not per tap.
  • The Guide is sent only the part of the player's state its line is about. Code picks the part by matching words before the model is called.
  • Group questions use no model.
  • A zero-token practice Sym is the fallback and the baseline.
  • Prompt caching worked for the Profiler (15 percent of its input was cached) and barely for the others, whose prompts are too short to qualify.
The experiment that answers "did a cut hurt": run the Twin on the same held-back cards twice for the same players, once on beliefs and once on all raw picks, and report tokens and matches for both. Also compare the AI Twin to the zero-token practice Sym on real players.

6Safety

The main defense is a design choice: no one can type to anyone else, and almost no one can type to a model. Questions, jobs and cards are tapped from fixed lists. The one place a player types (a line to their own Sym) is guarded before and after the model, is never stored, and reaches no other player.

What the game costs against how it is done today

To ask 194 people one question on a paid panel at the recommended rate, a company pays for each person's time plus about 43 percent in fees, and waits for the answers. In BYT the same question to 194 Syms returns at once, uses no tokens, and the agency's play credits go to the people behind the Syms. The saving is not that people are paid less. It is that a person is asked once and their Sym is asked many times.

8Limits and next steps

What does not work yet

  • No independent users yet. The accuracy and feedback numbers are from test accounts.
  • The crowd is mostly bots. 193 of the Syms an agency can ask are BOT Syms with random tastes. They make the economy visible. Their answers are not evidence about people, and this report does not use them as such.
  • A group of 5 can still hold one real person. The minimum counts BOT Syms. The rule that asks real people only in a crowd of 5 real people is written and tested but switched off, because with one real player it would stop that player earning.
  • No way back after logging out. There is no password or recovery key, by choice, so that no email is stored.
  • Certificates cannot be checked by an outside party.
  • The brain picture is a picture. The areas are loose textbook links. The game measures no brain and makes no health claim.
  • Rule numbers are choices, not findings: the 5-Sym minimum, the 10 percent share, the price of a repeat ask, the pay for a skill step.
  • Not yet tested on a phone, with a screen reader or by keyboard alone.
  • Being different earns nothing by itself. The game shows how a code differs but does not pay for it. That is deliberate: paying for rarity alone would reward random or hostile answers.

What comes next

  • A calibrator across every area of the mind, so the first ten taps write a code that is already a person's own.
  • Pay for what is rare and proved. A bonus for answers that differ from the crowd, only for a Sym that holds a certificate.
  • The history of change. Every block is dated. Reading how groups shift over time, and what moved them, is what an AI system cannot get anywhere else.
  • More kinds of game, built by creators from what Syms and agencies need.
  • Agencies that act on an answer: reach a real person to hire, with that person's consent each time.
  • Signed certificates, identity tiers and real money, in that order, each with its own review.

On its own a Sym is black and white. Color comes from other people.
